# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file.
The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and the project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- `murex.fusain`: drawing with dry and semi-dry media (graphite and coloured
  pencils, charcoal, soft, hard and oil pastels, wax crayons, ballpoint pens,
  stumps, erasers and a knife) on a paper with tooth. It is
  [fusain](https://github.com/joce/fusain), which was a library of its own
  built on murex and was never released: it moves here with its history, its
  tests (`tests/fusain/`), its tools (`tools/fusain/`), its examples and their
  images (`examples/fusain/`) and its documents (`docs/fusain/`: its README,
  its design, and its changelog up to the move). murex is becoming one package
  for every medium, so that media can one day meet on one sheet; oil paint
  (`murex.huile`) is next. `from fusain import Sheet` is now
  `from murex.fusain import Sheet`. It has no version of its own
  (`fusain.__version__` is gone: `murex.__version__` is the version).
  Its pictures are unchanged: every recorded hash of
  `tools/fusain/fingerprint.py` is as it was, and every image of
  `examples/fusain/output/` is regenerated byte for byte. `import murex` does
  not import it, and so compiles none of its kernels.

- `murex.huile`: oil painting. It is
  [oilpaint](https://github.com/joce/oilpaint), which moves here with its
  history, its tests (`tests/huile/`), its benchmark (`tools/huile/bench.py`),
  its examples and their images (`examples/huile/`) and its documents
  (`docs/huile/`: its README, its changelog up to the move, and the notes of
  what it gained since its release 0.1.0, which ships here first).
  `from oilpaint import Canvas` is now `from murex.huile import Canvas`. It
  has no version of its own (`oilpaint.__version__` is gone). oilpaint 0.1.0
  stays on PyPI and is not updated any more. A canvas, a brush or a paint
  pickled by oilpaint names its classes as `oilpaint.…` and does not load
  here, where they are `murex.huile.…`. Its pictures are unchanged:
  `tools/huile/bench.py --compare` against the states kept from oilpaint's
  last commit is identical on all 16 scenes, paint and picture, and every
  image of `examples/huile/output/` is regenerated byte for byte. The colour
  science, the paths, the noise, the reference images and the PNG writer it
  uses are murex's, no longer copies of its own: `from murex.huile import
  spectral, Reference, CatmullRom, Bezier, Polyline, mix_colors` work as
  before (they are murex's), but `murex.huile.spline`, `.noise`, `.reference`
  and `.png` are not modules any more (`murex.spline` and so on are). A
  `Paint` is a `murex.Pigment` with a body, and a `Pigment` that is no paint
  is taken wherever a paint is, as paint from the tube.

### Changed

- **Breaking, in huile's pictures:** a loaded knife pulled along the end
  ridge of an earlier pull no longer drops tall lumps (murex-6fa). A blade
  pushes along the wet paint that stands above it, and glides on its own
  fresh paint: under more than a film of that (0.3 units) it dragged
  nothing, and that went for the whole column of paint below, the part
  standing above the blade too. So a heap the blade was pushing stopped dead
  as soon as it came under such a film, and the blade went on without it.
  Along a ridge that happened in two ways, traced on the dark ground of
  `tools/huile/knife_probe.py` (pulls of a knife 160 px long loaded with 5,
  at pressure 0.7, each over the ridge the one before left at its blade's
  end; a dark of body 0.7 on 420 by 300 px):
  - Further down the pull, where the earlier ridge sinks under the blade's
    level. The heap rides some 9 px behind the leading edge of a blade 32 px
    wide, and from there on the blade lays its own paint in the gap ahead of
    it. In the second pull the heap, 6.4 to 7.1 units thick with the blade
    3.3 above the canvas, met a film 0.3 to 0.6 thick at the 19th step and
    stayed there, 10.6 and then 11.3 units thick (9.3 once the paint had
    settled).
  - At the pull's start, where the blade widens out of its taper and the
    paint it squeezes out past its end lands on the earlier ridge (0.5 to
    1.7 units, in the first step of the third pull). Over the next three
    steps the paint under those few pixels grew from 6.7 units to 10.2,
    13.6 and 17.6: a lump 10.7 thick once settled.

  The same in the flow alone: a blade pushing a ridge of 12 units of paint
  along 2.4 units of wet paint, come to where half a unit of its own film
  lies up to it, left the ridge 3 px into the film, 19 units high (8 under a
  blade that rides on the paint), in steps of 8, 2 or 1 px alike.

  Under its own film the blade still drags nothing, but it now pushes on
  what is in its way there (`_engine.pushed_under_film`): the paint it has
  brought there (what lies above where the wet paint lay before the
  stroke) and the paint that stands above the blade itself, as far as the
  surface, film and all, stands above the blade. The film is held down by
  the blade, so the paint that lifts it past the blade has to go on. The
  paint that lay there before the stroke and under the blade stays, however
  much of the stroke's own paint stands on it (the bead at a blade's end).
  The push grows from nothing with what is in the way: no new constant, and
  the 0.3 no longer decides whether a heap is let go. The flow under a blade
  is told how thick the wet paint lay before the stroke
  (`flow_under_blade` and `flow_under_riding_blade` take it as `before`).
  A heap now goes on under the film with the blade, or, under a blade that
  rides on it and so higher, is laid out in the room that leaves under the
  film: in the flow alone, 38 units stay with a blade held at its level and
  nothing behind it stands above the bed; a riding blade lays the 12 units
  out over 60 px, 0.6 above the bed at most.

  Two other ways of doing it were tried and measured. Pushing the paint on
  as far as the surface stands above the blade, whatever the surface is
  made of, moves the paint under the blade's own slab whenever the blade
  dips: a knife 110 px long loaded with 3, at pressure 0.5, then carried
  the wet paint under it 12% further than before (1% with what was done).
  And how the push joins the smoothing of the flow's speeds matters both
  ways: passing on the smoothed share alone left the paint standing on
  every thread top of the canvas (the surface ribbed by up to half a unit,
  4 px apart), and leaving the push out of the smoothing held a heap back
  at the film's edge (a lump 7.6 units thick left on the larger ground). It
  is smoothed with the speeds, and a pixel passes on no less than its own.

- **Breaking, in huile's pictures:** how far a knife drags wet paint no
  longer hangs on how its stroke is cut into steps (murex-kau). A blade
  pulled through wet paint pushes a wave of it along, as long as the blade
  is wide, and rides on it. Three things a step did once, or by its own
  length, made a stroke's own steps (8 px for a wide blade) drag, heap up
  and lay other amounts than short ones:
  - How high the blade rides was read once per step, from the paint under
    where the step takes it: a step further on, where the part of the wave
    it has yet to push there is missing. So the level was too low all along
    the step, the more the longer the step next to the blade's width, and
    too low a blade drags more. Measured over 2.8 units of wet dark, as how
    far above the level a step read the blade now ends the step, on average
    along a pull: 0.20, 0.12 and 0.02 units at pressures 0.25, 0.5 and 0.7
    for a blade 22 px wide in steps of 8 px (0.04 in steps of 4 px at
    pressure 0.25, nothing in steps of 2), 0.09, 0.75 and 1.02 units for one
    12 px wide, 0.02 at most for one 32 px wide. The flow under a blade that
    rides on wet paint (`_engine.flow_under_riding_blade`) now works its
    level out again as it goes, about every pixel of its way, from the paint
    under where the blade is then, as `_knife._blade_rest` works it out for
    a step (a pixel counting by how much of it the blade is over). The
    step's own reading still says whether the paint reaches the blade and
    what it pushes past its ends, and is the level of a blade that drags
    nothing. What a step
    does once the flow is done, it does at the level the blade ends the step
    at: the layers it folds together (`_knife._fold_layers`) are those it
    cuts into from there, where they were those under the level read ahead.
  - That flow took sub-steps of up to a pixel, whatever fitted the step:
    0.99 px (of the fastest paint, along x and y together) at a stroke's own
    steps along a slanted pull, 0.68 in steps of a pixel. The flow passes
    paint on from pixel to pixel, which spreads it along the way, the more
    the shorter the sub-steps, and the blade wavers on its wave the more the
    sharper the wave is: so the surface a pull left depended on which
    sub-steps its steps happened to take (at pressure 0.5, 0.026, 0.042,
    0.070 and 0.026 units of ripple in steps 1, 2, 4 and 8 times shorter;

    riding blade the sub-steps are now 0.7 px at most (`_engine.RIDE_CFL`),
    as near that as divides the step: 0.7 px down the slant of these pulls
    in steps of 8, 4, 2 and 1 px.
  - The blade laid the step's share of the mean of the gaps it met under it
    over the step. Where the gap opens as the blade passes, behind the wave,
    that is less than it lays in it step after step: 6, 8 and 12% less
    white at pressures 0.5, 0.7 and 0.9 than in steps eight times shorter
    (5 and 8% with the level alone put right). It now fills the gap
    sub-step by sub-step where it rode on the paint it dragged
    (`_engine.blade_slab`, `filled_as_met`): each part of the step fills its
    share of what is left of the gap it finds; a gap that does not change is
    filled as it was.

### Known, not changed

- Some results still follow the processor to the last bit: NumPy's `power` and
  `tanh` on arrays take a SIMD path on processors with AVX-512 (`tanh`, one
  without AVX2) that rounds some values differently from libm, so `srgb_to_linear`, `linear_to_srgb`, `solve_absorption` (and the optics
  of a `Pigment`) are not the same on every machine, and a BLAS with fused
  multiply-add sums `linear_rgb_to_reflectance` differently from one without.
  The media's kernels, their random draws and `noise.py` follow the machine
  likewise (numba's `math` is the C library's). A picture is therefore the
  same to the bit on one machine, and may differ in its last bits on another.
  Making every machine agree was done and undone before any release
  (`murex-krb`, `murex-9v4`): it takes murex's own maths library and random
  draws, about 900 lines with NumPy's ziggurat tables, and a ban on ordinary
  NumPy calls in murex's code, which is more than this project is worth.
  Recorded hashes are those of the machine that recorded them; fusain's
  fingerprint test and the pins of `tests/test_bits.py` skip elsewhere.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-15

Faster, with every result as it was: nothing here is a breaking change. What
`spectral` and `spline` return is the same to the last bit as in 0.1.1 for the
same arguments, which `tests/test_exact.py` checks against the code of 0.1.1.

### Added

- `spectral.km_layer` works out a batch of layers in compiled code: `kappa`
  (..., bands) and `sigma` (...) float64 arrays of two dimensions or more,
  over one ground (bands,) or over a ground for each layer (..., bands), as
  when every pixel of a picture has its own layer over what is already seen
  there. Five to six times as fast as numpy for a sheet of 1120 by 820
  pixels. Any other arguments are worked out by numpy, as before.
- `spectral.km_layer(..., out=)`: an array to put the result in, which may be
  the ground itself (a layer laid over what is seen, in place).

### Changed

- A `Path` works out its dense points once, at the first `sample()` or
  `length`, and keeps them; they were worked out at every call. It works
  them out again if its control points (or `alpha`, or `closed`) change, in
  place or not; a path whose `alpha` is an array keeps nothing. A subclass
  that implements `_dense` in its own way says what
  its dense points depend on in `_state`, or they are worked out at every
  call, as before.
- The dense points of a `CatmullRom`, the lengths along a path and the
  tangents of `sample()` are worked out in compiled code. Sampling the path
  of a short stroke and asking its length takes a tenth of the time.
- Where numpy warned of a result that is not a number (layers that are not
  finite, a Catmull-Rom path whose knots run into each other), the compiled
  code gives the same result without the warning.

## [0.1.1] - 2026-11-12

The first release. There is no 0.1.0: an unrelated project that once had this
name on PyPI used that version number in 2017, and PyPI never lets a file name
be used twice.

### Added

- The modules oilpaint and fusain share, taken from oilpaint as they are:
  `spectral` (colour science and Kubelka-Munk mixing), `spline` (stroke paths
  and profiles), `noise` (textures), `reference` (reference images), `png`
  and `filters` (oilpaint's `_filters`, with public names).
