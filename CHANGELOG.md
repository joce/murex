## [Unreleased]

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-26

### Added

- `murex.huile.SKIM`: the pressure of a knife that barely touches (0.03),
  at which a loaded blade lays a thin sheet that breaks in patches (see
  Changed). `tools/huile/break_stats.py` measures how a knife's white
  breaks over a dark, on a photograph of a painting or on what huile lays:
  the share covered, the holes and islands, the chords along the pull and
  across it, what a disc can reach, the correlation lengths and the peak a
  weave's lattice leaves.

- `murex.fusain`: Conté crayons (`conte.BLACK`, `conte.SANGUINE`,
  `conte.SEPIA`, `conte.BISTRE`, `conte.WHITE`, and `conte.crayon(colour)` for
  any other) and pastel pencils (`pastels.pencil(colour)`). Both are presets of
  the existing `Medium`: no mechanism is new, and no existing medium, hash of
  `tools/fusain/fingerprint.py` or image changes. A Conté crayon is firmer
  than a hard pastel and holds better: over two firm layers it lays 0.63 of
  what a hard pastel lays, 0.42 of it lies as dust against 0.60, a finger
  carries off three quarters as much, and a kneaded eraser pulled three times
  leaves 0.41 of it against 0.32. A pastel pencil lies between the two (0.80
  of a hard pastel, 0.51 dust), and its side is the cone of a sharpened core,
  whose band fades to its edges, where a stick's is even. These figures follow
  from a softness and a binding guessed from how the real sticks behave; they
  are not measured against them. `examples/fusain/conte.py` draws a pear and
  an egg aux trois crayons (black, sanguine and white on `papers.TONED_TAN`).

- `murex.fusain`: drawing with dry and semi-dry media (graphite and coloured
  pencils, charcoal, soft, hard and oil pastels, wax crayons, ballpoint pens,
  stumps, erasers and a knife) on a paper with tooth. It is
  [fusain](https://github.com/joce/fusain), which was a library of its own
  built on murex and was never released: it moves here with its history, its
  tests (`tests/fusain/`), its tools (`tools/fusain/`), its examples and their
  images (`examples/fusain/`) and its documents (`docs/fusain/`: its README,
  its design, and its changelog up to the move). murex is becoming one package
  for every medium, so that media can one day meet on one sheet; oil paint
  (`murex.huile`) is next. `from fusain import Sheet` is now
  `from murex.fusain import Sheet`. It has no version of its own
  (`fusain.__version__` is gone: `murex.__version__` is the version).
  Its pictures are unchanged: every recorded hash of
  `tools/fusain/fingerprint.py` is as it was, and every image of
  `examples/fusain/output/` is regenerated byte for byte. `import murex` does
  not import it, and so compiles none of its kernels.

- `murex.huile`: oil painting. It is
  [oilpaint](https://github.com/joce/oilpaint), which moves here with its
  history, its tests (`tests/huile/`), its benchmark (`tools/huile/bench.py`),
  its examples and their images (`examples/huile/`) and its documents
  (`docs/huile/`: its README, its changelog up to the move, and the notes of
  what it gained since its release 0.1.0, which ships here first).
  `from oilpaint import Canvas` is now `from murex.huile import Canvas`. It
  has no version of its own (`oilpaint.__version__` is gone). oilpaint 0.1.0
  stays on PyPI and is not updated any more. A canvas, a brush or a paint
  pickled by oilpaint names its classes as `oilpaint.…` and does not load
  here, where they are `murex.huile.…`. Its pictures are unchanged:
  `tools/huile/bench.py --compare` against the states kept from oilpaint's
  last commit is identical on all 16 scenes, paint and picture, and every
  image of `examples/huile/output/` is regenerated byte for byte. The colour
  science, the paths, the noise, the reference images and the PNG writer it
  uses are murex's, no longer copies of its own: `from murex.huile import
  spectral, Reference, CatmullRom, Bezier, Polyline, mix_colors` work as
  before (they are murex's), but `murex.huile.spline`, `.noise`, `.reference`
  and `.png` are not modules any more (`murex.spline` and so on are). A
  `Paint` is a `murex.Pigment` with a body, and a `Pigment` that is no paint
  is taken wherever a paint is, as paint from the tube.

### Changed

- **Breaking, in huile's pictures of tools held at a fixed angle:** with
  `follow_path=False` a brush's or a blade's long side lies at the `angle`
  given, on every heading (murex-1w8). The angle is counted from the
  horizontal, counter-clockwise as the picture is seen: the way a tool
  turns with `follow_path=True`, and the way a level or an upright stroke
  and a dab took it already. On any other heading the tool lay twice that
  heading away: at 90 degrees for a blade asked to lie level on a pull
  down a slope of 45. Callers had allowed for it by giving the angle the
  other way round (a blade across a pull heading h, in the path's
  coordinates, as `h - 90`): such an angle is now given as `90 - h`,
  and the stroke is then the same to the bit. So:
  - a stroke on a slant (heading h, in the path's coordinates) with its
    angle left as it was is painted with the tool turned twice h: give
    `angle - 2h` to have the tool where it lay, which for a blade across
    its way is the angle negated;
  - an upright stroke (drawn up or down) is unchanged to the bit;
  - a level stroke and a dab keep their angle, with the tool turned half
    a turn, its sides swapped: the same mark with another texture. Add
    180 to the angle to have it to the bit.

  `tools/huile/bench.py --compare` is identical on its 16 scenes, none of
  which holds a tool so. In `tests/huile/` the pulls across a slanting way
  give their angle as `90 - h` and the scrape of the shared dark ground
  as -90 where it was 90, and no value held by a test changes but those
  of `tests/huile/test_relief.py`, whose pull is given the angle that was
  meant (40: its -40 lay mirrored about its way) and lays 0.2% more white
  at pressures 0.1 and 0.3; `tools/huile/knife_probe.py` gives 54
  where it gave -54 and measures what it did. Of the example images two
  change: `brush_uses.png` (the calligraphy loop of
  `examples/huile/demo.py`, whose nib now keeps its 40 degrees all round
  the loops; the line is as thick and as thin where it was, its ends and
  its texture are not) and `knives.png` (the grass of its meadow, blades
  dabbed at 90 degrees give or take 14: each leans as it did, turned
  half a turn, so its texture is another).

- **Breaking, in huile's pictures of skims:** a knife barely touching lays
  white that breaks in patches, as snow goes on a mountain (murex-dnj).
  Below pressure 0.07 the bead on a loaded blade's edge comes to lay as a
  roll of stiff paint, and up to 0.04 as that alone: a thin sheet of one
  thickness with ragged holes in it, where it laid specks a thread across
  on the lattice of the weave. `murex.huile.SKIM` (0.03) names that
  pressure: `canvas.stroke(path, brush=knife, pressure=SKIM)`. **Every
  stroke pressed 0.07 or harder all along is what it was, to the bit**, and
  so are brushes, combs, dabs of a knife and clean blades:
  `tools/huile/bench.py --compare` is identical on its 16 scenes, and no
  example painting but `bob_ross_style.py` was found to have a knife below
  0.07 (by reading them: they were not run).

  **The rule** (`_knife._Roll`). The roll reads the surface as it lay
  before the stroke, smoothed over its own width (5 px: the threads are
  lost in it); its edge is straight over 10 px either way along the blade
  and rests on the high places there, and it feels 0.6 of the hollow under
  that edge; it reaches 0.3 units below it, and 8 more per unit of
  pressure, less a wander of 0.55 units (standard deviation) in cells of 16
  px along the pull by 5 across, drawn for each stroke from a generator of
  its own (no other texture of any stroke is drawn differently, and it
  draws for the length of the path, not for its steps). The wander strays
  as far at every place: value noise as it comes strays more at the
  corners of its cells than midway (0.61 against 0.48 units), which made a
  hole 1.56 times likelier every 16 px along every pull, so each value is
  divided by how far values stray at its place, and the cells begin
  anywhere. Where it reaches
  the surface it lays, over 0.15 units of reach, the paint of a bead
  pressed 0.091 against the surface: 0.70 units under a blade 110 px long
  (0.56 under one of 70 px, 0.41 under one of 40: a shorter blade passes
  over a place sooner, as for the bead before). All of it is worked out
  before the stroke, over the stroke (rows 1 px along its path, columns 1
  px across), and a step only reads it where its pixels lie: nothing is
  kept along the stroke. Between 0.04 and 0.07 the bead's catching on
  thread tops comes in evenly and the roll goes out later
  (`_ROLL_LATE`), by the pressure at each place along the stroke, and at
  each place the bead leaves the larger of what the two would, not their
  sum. The sheet is that thick under a blade with paint to spare: with a
  film thinner than 10 units on it (`_ROLL_FILM`; a 110 px blade loaded
  with 8 carries 40) the roll is pressed in proportion, and a load of 1
  lays a sheet 0.3 units thick.

  **Measured** with the new `tools/huile/break_stats.py` on the picture, at
  about 2 px/mm, against the eleven bounds of murex-dnj (taken from eight
  faces of three paintings), one pull at pressure 0.03 over a wet dark.
  How many pulls meet all eleven depends on the dark:
  - *knifed thin* (the default canvas, LINEN_COARSE, LINEN_FINE,
    COTTON_DUCK, GESSO_PANEL with and without its tooth, OIL_PAPER, and
    dried): every pull measured, 84 in three sets (36, 36 and 12, with
    other seeds, canvases and faces). The cover is 0.74 to 0.85.
  - *brushed rough* (the default canvas, LINEN_COARSE, COTTON_DUCK,
    CANVAS_BOARD; dabbed on with a round; dried): 35 of 36 in one set, 26 of
    36 in another (other seeds on the grounds of `tests/huile/
    test_skim.py`, a smaller face), 8 of 9 in a third. What falls outside:
    the dark chord across the pull, 4.5 to 5.3 mm where 4.5 is the most
    asked (11 pulls), and the white in two pieces, its largest island 0.54
    to 0.70 of it where 0.85 is asked (4 pulls). The cover is 0.65 to
    0.76. The test holds three pulls a ground to bounds widened to 0.75
    and 5.0 mm, which 7 of those 36 other pulls fall outside too: its
    bounds are those of its own pulls, not of every pull.
  - *crossed by a knife's lips*, thin: 6 of 6.
  - *knifed thick* (loaded with 6 at pressures 0.2 and 0.35, the lips of
    its pulls standing in it):
    none of 6. The white is not one sheet there: it follows the ridges of
    the slab (0.42 to 0.48 of the face covered, 0.22 to 0.36 of the white
    in its largest island).

  At a stroke's own steps, in steps of 2 px and of half a pixel, a pull
  measures the same (78 of 78 on thirteen grounds, two pulls each). As it
  was, such a pull met one or two of the eleven. *A second pull over the
  same place* fills most of the first one's holes: over a dark knifed
  thin 0.88 to 0.96 is covered, most pairs over the 0.90 that is the most
  asked; over a brushed one 0.81 to 0.89.
  It has holes of its own, and only 0.6 of how much lower the first one's
  lie counts against it. On an even ground the holes are the stroke's
  own, the same on any even ground for the same seed: that is meant.

  **At every cut** the same skim lays the same paint: in steps of 8, 4, 2
  and 1 px against half a pixel, within 0.003% in all and 0.32% place by
  place (root mean square, smoothed over 7 px) on the default canvas and
  LINEN_COARSE, over a dark knifed thin and over one brushed rough; the
  bead as it was, 0.03 to 0.11% and 0.4 to 0.6% (`tests/huile/
  test_knife_cuts.py`). (The study behind this rule found its prototype
  1.3% and 6% off on the brushed LINEN_COARSE and the old bead 84%: that
  was before a stroke's textures were the same at every cut, murex-9t4;
  with them the old bead is 0.1% and 0.9% off there.) A pull pressed from
  0.02 to 0.09 along its way is 0.30% and 1.55% off at a stroke's own
  steps, with the roll or without: past 0.05 a blade spreads its film by
  each step's own pressure, as it did. At the edge of the canvas a skim
  paints as on a larger canvas (`tests/huile/test_past_edge.py`), moved by
  whole pixels it is the same stroke to the bit, and it keeps its paint.

  **Against pressure** a pull does not always lay more the harder it is
  pressed, and cannot while 0.07 is left as it is: from there a blade lays
  as it did, in places less than its roll lays at 0.04, and no way from
  the one to the other then rises all the way. Two things keep the fall
  small (murex-cwa). Between 0.04 and 0.07 the bead leaves, at each
  place, the larger of what the roll and the catching would, not their
  sum, and the roll goes out late (`_ROLL_LATE`, 2.5): summed, a roll
  kept late lay on top of the catching and a pull laid more midway than
  at 0.07, and let go early it left a dip. And the roll of a blade with
  a film under 10 units is pressed in proportion (`_ROLL_FILM`): such a
  blade laid all it had as a skim, and less pressed harder. A skim at
  0.04 or under with a load of 8 is what it was before these two, to the
  bit. Measured from 0 to 0.1 in steps of 0.0025, blades of 40 and 110
  px, loads of 1, 3 and 8 (and 20 under the 110 px blade), the furthest
  the white falls below what a lighter pull laid (two to four pulls each:
  the fall varies with the stroke's seed), and in brackets what it was
  with the two summed and the roll pressed alike under every blade:

  | ground | load 1 | load 3 | loads 8 and 20 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | dark knifed thin; brushed rough on LINEN_COARSE; bare default canvas | none (1.5 to 7.5%) | none (none) | none (none) |
  | bare LINEN_COARSE | 0 to 0.4% (10 to 20%) | 3 to 10% (7 to 14%) | none (0.2 to 3%) |
  | no tooth (GESSO_PANEL made with `texture=0`), bare | 35 to 66% (53 to 79%) | 41 to 58% (53 to 64%) | 9 to 25% (27 to 39%) |

  What is left on a ground with no tooth is mostly the old way itself:
  loaded with 8 a 110 px blade lays 15% less at 0.07 than its roll does
  at 0.04, and loaded with 3, 57% less, whatever lies between. (A dark
  brushed on the default canvas, a thick slab and a thin dark over a
  ground with no tooth were measured before these two and not again.)
  With very little paint: the pull of `tests/huile/test_relief.py` (a 40
  px knife loaded with 0.22, over dried ridges) lays 164 units at 0.04,
  193 at 0.06, 172 at 0.07, 197 at 0.1 and 258 at 0.3 (with the roll
  pressed alike under every blade, 312 at 0.04: more than at any pressure
  up to 0.3; before the roll it rose all the way from 97); loaded with 2,
  1493 at 0.06 and 1416 at 0.07 (1775 at 0.05). The share of a face
  covered still falls where the white does not: a 40 px blade loaded
  with 3 covers as much of its face up to 0.0625 as a skim does and half
  of that at 0.0675 (it was half from 0.0625 on), and a 110 px blade
  loaded with 1, at its worst, 0.5 to 0.57 of the face less than as a
  skim (0.40 to 0.47 before: its skim then ran dry along the way), where
  the sheet has given way to specks on thread tops.
  `tests/huile/test_skim.py` holds a pull to rising all the way over two
  darks at loads of 3 and 8, and holds five pulls on bare grounds to
  what was measured (two of them to rising all the way), so that a
  worsening is caught. (The roll going out as the catching comes in, the
  two summing to 1, there was a fall of 1.2 to 1.6% on the bare default
  canvas as well.)

  **Cost.** Working a roll out takes 9 ms for a pull 270 px long under a
  blade 110 px wide (18 ms where it reaches past the edge), and reading it
  next to nothing: a skimming pull takes 0 to 4% longer (345 to 360 ms),
  one between 0.04 and 0.07 up to 10% (it lays more), and pulls at 0.1 and
  0.25 the same within the 2% the timing wavers by.

  **Changed with it.** `examples/huile/bob_ross_style.py` lays its snow so:
  one pull of the knife at `SKIM` down each face of a ridge from its peak,
  the blade turning with the ridge line, loaded with about what the pull
  lays and lifted off as it goes, so that the white gives out in streaks;
  a second lower on a long face; the shadow side first (156 strokes on its
  mountains where there were 413). Its mountain's dark is laid with half
  the paint, each pull begun square to the blade, into the mountain, and
  then turned to come down: pulled straight down askew to its own edge, a
  blade left a bead of paint standing above the ridge line at its upper
  end, a row of dark posts that the old snow had covered. A pull eased
  off from 0.1 to 0.02 over dried ridges with very little paint lays 178
  units where it laid 150 (`tests/huile/test_relief.py`). The sizes above
  are pixels: huile has no
  scale to turn millimetres into pixels by (its surfaces assume 0.3 mm to
  the pixel, and this rule was set at 0.5).

  **Not changed, and seen while at it:** a blade's two ends print a
  straight rail across an earlier pull; ribs across a pull (murex-bja); a
  comb's cut (murex-ffp).

- **Breaking, in huile's pictures:** a stroke's random textures no longer
  show how it is cut into steps (murex-9t4). The wobble of a stroke's width
  counted its noise in steps (`value_noise_1d(len(pts) + 2, 0.8 * w /
  spacing)`), so a stroke cut otherwise drew more or fewer random numbers
  for it, and every texture drawn after it (a knife's chatter, the swell of
  its lips, the tilt of its blade, its colour bands, the hair or the nicks)
  was another, as a different seed gives it. The hair also covered a length
  and a width measured on the points of the cut (`arc[-1]`,
  `radii.max()`), so its array changed shape from cut to cut even where the
  draws were the same; a brush's load was measured in the mean width of the
  cut's points, and how far before its end it starts lifting off in their
  greatest radius.

  Measured before the change, the same stroke with its steps capped at 8,
  4, 2 and 1 px (half a pixel for knives), five seeds, on the bare canvas,
  on thick brushed wet paint and on a knifed wet slab, each cap against
  the finest. At a stroke's own steps the chatter and the lips were
  unrelated to the finest cut's for every tool tried, knives 60 and 110 px
  wide, a 30 px hog flat, a 16 px round and a 24 px filbert (root mean
  square difference 1.2 to 2.9 times the texture's own spread, where 1.4
  is two independent draws), the tilt differed by up to 0.23, the bands
  were another draw and the hair another array; the knives' wobble was
  unrelated too (1.7 to 1.8), the brushes' off by 0.2 to 0.3 of its
  spread. With the cap at 4, 2 or 1 px it depended on the tool: the 60 px
  knife had other draws at every cap, the 110 px knife and the filbert at
  4, and where the draws were the same the hair was still another array
  for all but the hog flat. The paint showed it: a 110 px knife skimming
  at pressure 0.05 kept 0.65 to 1.81 times as much paint on its blade at
  its own steps as in steps of half a pixel, and the wet paint it left
  on the bare canvas differed from the finest cut's by 70 to 88% of
  itself with the cap at 8 and at 4 (the 60 px knife's by 27 to 54% at
  every cap; 1 to 3% over wet grounds).

  Now every texture is noise against the distance along the path (px):
  as many numbers are drawn as the length of the path calls for
  (`whole_floor` and `whole_ceil`, from `Path.length`, which no cut
  exceeds), and each point reads them where it lies
  (`stroke._stroke_textures`; the new `murex.noise.value_noise_at` reads
  a line of random values at any places, as `value_noise_1d` reads its
  own at whole steps, to the bit). What the hair covers, the width a load
  is measured in and how far before its end a brush starts lifting off
  are taken from the stroke cut into its own steps (`stroke._Own`: a
  quarter of its width, 8 px at most), whatever steps it is painted in. A
  stroke painted in its own steps is its own reference: it is not sampled
  twice.

  After the change, on the three grounds, each cap against the finest:
  the generator is left in the same state, the tilt, the bands and the
  hair are the same arrays at the same place (to the bit), and the wobble,
  the chatter and the lips are the same along the length, read at the
  cut's own points (0.8% of the texture's spread for a knife's wobble at
  its own steps, 4% for its chatter, whose turns are 24 px apart for
  steps of 8). A 60 px knife then leaves the same wet paint within 3.3%
  on the bare canvas (it was 27 to 54%) and 0.1% over wet grounds, and
  keeps as much on its blade within 2.2% (it was 0.90 to 1.20 times); the
  110 px skim within 0.4%, and 0.7% on its blade (it was 70 to 88%, and
  0.65 to 1.81 times). `tests/huile/test_textures_along.py` checks it for
  two knives, a flat and a soft round whose width and pressure vary, on a
  curve and on a bend, that a second stroke does not show how the first
  was cut, and that the draws do not hang on a last bit of the length.

  **What an ordinary stroke loses or gains.** A stroke that is not cut
  finer than its own steps keeps its character: the wobble is as strong
  as it was (the same noise, 3% of the width either way) and turns every
  0.8 widths, which is what it did at a stroke's own steps. It is drawn
  for the path's length in whole steps of the stroke's own and three
  more, as it was when it was counted in steps: so of the 2251 strokes
  and marks of the 16 benchmark scenes, 2101 leave the generator as they
  left it, with the tilt and the bands they had, and a wobble stretched
  by a few percent at most (its steps are a little shorter than the
  nominal one). Most of those keep their hair too, not all: the
  stretched wobble can change the widest the bundle is, and with it how
  many columns the hair's array has, and a few have another array of the
  same draws (the review of this change counted about 22 strokes that
  are not cut finer and get another hair array). The other 150 are as
  another seed would have made them: 118 in `painting`, 12 in
  `taper-curve`, 8 in `fan`, 7 in `edges`, 4 in `round-small`, 1 in
  `liner`. All 16 scenes change: the paint that lies elsewhere than it
  did is 0.1 to 0.2% of all of it in `knife`, `knife-lift` and
  `flat-wide`, 1.2% in `taper-curve` and in `painting`. The strokes whose
  character does change are those of any tool that is not round, where
  they turn: a flat's, a filbert's, a fan's, and a knife's on a curve.
  They are cut finer there, and their width wobbled as many times faster
  as they were cut finer (up to 23 times); it now wobbles as any
  stroke's does. (Measured in the review: the width of a 60 px knife on
  a gentle curve crossed its mean every 4.5 px before, and every 100 to
  106 px now.) A
  dab reads the wobble where its two points lie, half a pixel apart or
  so, not a step apart (0.15% less paint in the test that pins one).
  Setting a stroke going takes as long as it did (a short round: 341 and
  348 us before, 353 and 342 after; a long flat: 1.41 and 1.42 ms
  before, 1.40 and 1.38 after); on the benchmark's 16 scenes no
  difference can be told from the machine's noise, which was up to 20%
  between two runs of the same code.

  **The tests that matched the textures.** The `same_textures` fixture
  is gone: it switched the wobble off and drew 8 numbers in its place, so
  that cuts could be compared. The tests that took it, in five files, now
  run with each stroke's own textures, and so with another tilt, other
  bands and other nicks than the fixture left them. How near a knife's
  cuts come to each other depends on those, which was already so: the
  bounds were those of the one pull the fixture left (with the fixture's
  draws the new code gives the old figures: checked, the slab on the bare
  canvas to all printed digits, the pull over brushed paint to 121,500
  and 4,245). The slab a heavily loaded 110 px blade lays on the bare
  canvas was, over six such pulls with the wobble off, 2.2% thinner to
  0.9% thicker at a stroke's own steps than in steps of half a pixel (the
  test allowed 1%, and its pull gave 0.1%); with their own textures six
  pulls give 0.8 to 2.5% thinner (the test now allows 3% for its pull,
  which gives 2.2%). A heavily loaded 60 px knife carries the wet paint
  1.2% further at most and leaves 2.6% more beside it (2% and 4% allowed,
  where 1% and 3% were). Two bounds on a level, not on a cut, are
  wider for the same reason (the ripple down a slant, 0.066 units where
  the fixture's pull gave 0.055; the heap where a pull stops, 7.7 units,
  which the test's docstring already gave for a stroke's own textures),
  and the load a curve lays next to a straight stroke is now the average
  of six strokes each (it hung on a seed: 0.76 to 1.13 from seed to seed
  before, 0.93 to 1.18 now). Three pinned values are measured again (a
  dab's volume, the cover of a ridge beside a 32 px flat, what a clean
  knife carries over brushed paint). `tools/huile/knife_probe.py --step`
  no longer switches the wobble off for `lumps`.

  **Not changed: a brush still depends on its steps.** With the textures
  the same at every cut it can be measured stroke by stroke. A brush lays
  as much paint at every cut (a 30 px hog flat, a 16 px round and a
  24 px filbert at their own steps against steps of 1 px: within 1.1% on
  the bare canvas and over brushed paint; 2.4 to 4.5% more for the flat
  over a knifed slab). What it lifts and carries off does depend on the
  cut: at its own steps it ends its stroke holding 0.72 to 1.02 times
  what it holds in steps of 1 px on the bare canvas, 0.27 to 0.67 times
  over brushed wet paint and 0.57 to 0.83 times over a knifed slab (in

- **Breaking, in huile's pictures:** the canvas goes on past its edge, and
  strokes are painted there (murex-n6o, murex-6s6, murex-w8r, murex-uwl).
  Past its edge a canvas is bare at first (its ground, and its weave over
  again) and then holds whatever strokes leave there. The steps of a
  stroke that reach past the edge, a brush's as well as a knife's, are
  painted on the canvas and what lies past its edge as one surface (a
  stage, `_knife._Stage`), and after the stroke the paint settles across
  the edge. So what strokes leave on the canvas is what the same strokes
  leave on a larger canvas. A stroke that stays clear of the edge is
  painted on the canvas itself and comes out as before, to the bit.

  What was wrong (after murex-duo, the next entry). Past the edge a knife
  met the canvas's mirror image, made anew for each stroke and gone after
  it, 128 px out at most; the paint settled on the canvas alone; and a
  brush past the edge laid nothing anywhere. So:
  - paint a blade had brought on lay out there again for the next blade,
    and paint it had pushed off was gone: over repeated strokes the edge
    was a source (murex-n6o);
  - the mirror image had paint where nobody had laid any (murex-6s6): over
    a ground brushed up to the edge and no further, a blade coming on
    brought on paint from outside;
  - thick paint left at the edge piled against it as against a wall
    (murex-w8r);
  - a blade from further out than 128 px dragged nothing till there
    (murex-uwl).

  How it is made.
  - `Canvas._past_edge` holds what lies past the edge: tiles 16 px square,
    where there is anything, each with the planes `top`, `und`, `top_a`,
    `und_a`, `top_id` and the height of the paint dried there. A canvas
    nothing is taken past the edge of holds none. It is copied and pickled
    with the canvas (a canvas pickled before loads with none), a render
    does not show it, and `dry()` dries it where it lies: a later blade
    rests on it (the maps of the relief past the edge are worked out from
    the weave and what has dried there). On the canvas, paint dried past
    the edge raises the relief beside it and nowhere else: the canvas's
    maps are worked out again, with what lies round the canvas, only as

  Other ways were weighed. Letting each edge pixel take in from past the
  edge what it passes on (the edge pixel repeated, at every sub-step)
  makes the edge a spring: as long as a blade is over it, it gives without
  end, where the paint past the edge has been dragged on and is not there
  any more; and it does nothing about where the blade rests out there, nor
  about bare canvas. Keeping the paint past the edge in tiles and
  gathering each step's window from them and the canvas is the stage,
  step by step: two copies of the window at every step for the same
  result. An earlier attempt read the surface past the edge as it was
  before the blade drained it (dropped): the paint past the edge stayed
  inert, 2,346 units of white landed where the larger canvas gets 564, and
  a band of white lay along the edge.

  the edge can reach, not the stroke: steps are painted on a stage only
  while their windows reach past the edge. So a long stroke that leaves
  over an edge takes little more than one that does not: across 2,000 by
  2,000 px, 80 MB at its peak for 67 before the change, in 3.2 s for 3.2;
  across 4,000 by 4,000 px, 295 MB for 282, in 8.5 s for 8.3. (One stage

  made before its first step, so that what lay past the edge was the mirror

  The derivations are kept (`spectral._build_spectrum_to_rgb`
  and `_build_basis`, and `tools/derive_spectral_tables.py`, which writes the
  module); a test checks that they agree with the tables to 1e-13.

- **Breaking, for anyone on NumPy 1.x:** NumPy 1.x is no longer supported. The
  requirement is `numpy>=2.0`, and numba's floor rises with it, from
  `numba>=0.57` to `numba>=0.60` (numba 0.57 to 0.59 do not support NumPy 2).
  The reason: `spectral.srgb_to_reflectance` returned different numbers under
  NumPy 1.26.4 and under NumPy 2.5.5 (same Python 3.12, numba 0.68.1 and
  murex 0.2.0; the reflectance of a colour hashed differently), and two
  libraries depend on these numbers, one of which checks its pictures byte for
  byte. CI and the lock only ever ran NumPy 2. No result under NumPy 2
  changes.

### Added

- `tests/test_layers.py` checks which way imports point: colour (`spectral`,
  `pigment`) imports nothing else of murex, the other shared modules import
  no medium, a medium imports no other medium, and importing `murex` imports
  no medium. `tests/test_rounding.py` nudges lengths by an ulp and checks that
  the counts stay.
- `tests/test_bits.py` pins reflectances, spectrum to RGB and Kubelka-Munk
  layers to the last bit, on x86-64. On ARM eight of them differ in the last
  bits, so on another kind of processor they are skipped.

### Known, not changed

- Some results still follow the processor to the last bit: NumPy's `power` and
  `tanh` on arrays take a SIMD path on processors with AVX-512 (`tanh`, one
  without AVX2) that rounds some values differently from libm, so `srgb_to_linear`, `linear_to_srgb`, `solve_absorption` (and the optics
  of a `Pigment`) are not the same on every machine, and a BLAS with fused
  multiply-add sums `linear_rgb_to_reflectance` differently from one without.
  The media's kernels, their random draws and `noise.py` follow the machine
  likewise (numba's `math` is the C library's). A picture is therefore the
  same to the bit on one machine, and may differ in its last bits on another.
  Making every machine agree was done and undone before any release
  (`murex-krb`, `murex-9v4`): it takes murex's own maths library and random
  draws, about 900 lines with NumPy's ziggurat tables, and a ban on ordinary
  NumPy calls in murex's code, which is more than this project is worth.
  Recorded hashes are those of the machine that recorded them; fusain's
  fingerprint test and the pins of `tests/test_bits.py` skip elsewhere.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-15

Faster, with every result as it was: nothing here is a breaking change. What
`spectral` and `spline` return is the same to the last bit as in 0.1.1 for the
same arguments, which `tests/test_exact.py` checks against the code of 0.1.1.

### Added

- `spectral.km_layer` works out a batch of layers in compiled code: `kappa`
  (..., bands) and `sigma` (...) float64 arrays of two dimensions or more,
  over one ground (bands,) or over a ground for each layer (..., bands), as
  when every pixel of a picture has its own layer over what is already seen
  there. Five to six times as fast as numpy for a sheet of 1120 by 820
  pixels. Any other arguments are worked out by numpy, as before.
- `spectral.km_layer(..., out=)`: an array to put the result in, which may be
  the ground itself (a layer laid over what is seen, in place).

### Changed

- A `Path` works out its dense points once, at the first `sample()` or
  `length`, and keeps them; they were worked out at every call. It works
  them out again if its control points (or `alpha`, or `closed`) change, in
  place or not; a path whose `alpha` is an array keeps nothing. A subclass
  that implements `_dense` in its own way says what
  its dense points depend on in `_state`, or they are worked out at every
  call, as before.
- The dense points of a `CatmullRom`, the lengths along a path and the
  tangents of `sample()` are worked out in compiled code. Sampling the path
  of a short stroke and asking its length takes a tenth of the time.
- Where numpy warned of a result that is not a number (layers that are not
  finite, a Catmull-Rom path whose knots run into each other), the compiled
  code gives the same result without the warning.

## [0.1.1] - 2026-11-12

The first release. There is no 0.1.0: an unrelated project that once had this
name on PyPI used that version number in 2017, and PyPI never lets a file name
be used twice.

### Added

- The modules oilpaint and fusain share, taken from oilpaint as they are:
  `spectral` (colour science and Kubelka-Munk mixing), `spline` (stroke paths
  and profiles), `noise` (textures), `reference` (reference images), `png`
  and `filters` (oilpaint's `_filters`, with public names).
- `Pigment`: oilpaint's `Paint` without what is particular to oil paint
  (its body).
