# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file.
The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and the project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- `murex.fusain`: drawing with dry and semi-dry media (graphite and coloured
  pencils, charcoal, soft, hard and oil pastels, wax crayons, ballpoint pens,
  stumps, erasers and a knife) on a paper with tooth. It is
  [fusain](https://github.com/joce/fusain), which was a library of its own
  built on murex and was never released: it moves here with its history, its
  tests (`tests/fusain/`), its tools (`tools/fusain/`), its examples and their
  images (`examples/fusain/`) and its documents (`docs/fusain/`: its README,
  its design, and its changelog up to the move). murex is becoming one package
  for every medium, so that media can one day meet on one sheet; oil paint
  (`murex.huile`) is next. `from fusain import Sheet` is now
  `from murex.fusain import Sheet`. It has no version of its own
  (`fusain.__version__` is gone: `murex.__version__` is the version).
  Its pictures are unchanged: every recorded hash of
  `tools/fusain/fingerprint.py` is as it was, and every image of
  `examples/fusain/output/` is regenerated byte for byte. `import murex` does
  not import it, and so compiles none of its kernels.

- `murex.huile`: oil painting. It is
  [oilpaint](https://github.com/joce/oilpaint), which moves here with its
  history, its tests (`tests/huile/`), its benchmark (`tools/huile/bench.py`),
  its examples and their images (`examples/huile/`) and its documents
  (`docs/huile/`: its README, its changelog up to the move, and the notes of
  what it gained since its release 0.1.0, which ships here first).
  `from oilpaint import Canvas` is now `from murex.huile import Canvas`. It
  has no version of its own (`oilpaint.__version__` is gone). oilpaint 0.1.0
  stays on PyPI and is not updated any more. A canvas, a brush or a paint
  pickled by oilpaint names its classes as `oilpaint.…` and does not load
  here, where they are `murex.huile.…`. Its pictures are unchanged:
  `tools/huile/bench.py --compare` against the states kept from oilpaint's
  last commit is identical on all 16 scenes, paint and picture, and every
  image of `examples/huile/output/` is regenerated byte for byte. The colour
  science, the paths, the noise, the reference images and the PNG writer it
  uses are murex's, no longer copies of its own: `from murex.huile import
  spectral, Reference, CatmullRom, Bezier, Polyline, mix_colors` work as
  before (they are murex's), but `murex.huile.spline`, `.noise`, `.reference`
  and `.png` are not modules any more (`murex.spline` and so on are). A
  `Paint` is a `murex.Pigment` with a body, and a `Pigment` that is no paint
  is taken wherever a paint is, as paint from the tube.

### Changed

- **Breaking, in huile's pictures:** the canvas goes on past its edge, and
  strokes are painted there (murex-n6o, murex-6s6, murex-w8r, murex-uwl).
  Past its edge a canvas is bare at first (its ground, and its weave over
  again) and then holds whatever strokes leave there. The steps of a
  stroke that reach past the edge, a brush's as well as a knife's, are
  painted on the canvas and what lies past its edge as one surface (a
  stage, `_knife._Stage`), and after the stroke the paint settles across
  the edge. So what strokes leave on the canvas is what the same strokes
  leave on a larger canvas. A stroke that stays clear of the edge is
  painted on the canvas itself and comes out as before, to the bit.

  What was wrong (after murex-duo, the next entry). Past the edge a knife
  met the canvas's mirror image, made anew for each stroke and gone after
  it, 128 px out at most; the paint settled on the canvas alone; and a
  brush past the edge laid nothing anywhere. So:
  - paint a blade had brought on lay out there again for the next blade,
    and paint it had pushed off was gone: over repeated strokes the edge
    was a source (murex-n6o);
  - the mirror image had paint where nobody had laid any (murex-6s6): over
    a ground brushed up to the edge and no further, a blade coming on
    brought on paint from outside;
  - thick paint left at the edge piled against it as against a wall
    (murex-w8r);
  - a blade from further out than 128 px dragged nothing till there
    (murex-uwl).

  How it is made.
  - `Canvas._past_edge` holds what lies past the edge: tiles 16 px square,
    where there is anything, each with the planes `top`, `und`, `top_a`,
    `und_a`, `top_id` and the height of the paint dried there. A canvas
    nothing is taken past the edge of holds none. It is copied and pickled
    with the canvas (a canvas pickled before loads with none), a render
    does not show it, and `dry()` dries it where it lies: a later blade
    rests on it (the maps of the relief past the edge are worked out from
    the weave and what has dried there). On the canvas, paint dried past
    the edge raises the relief beside it and nowhere else: the canvas's
    maps are worked out again, with what lies round the canvas, only as
    far from that paint as they read the surface (18 px, or a thread and a
    half of the weave and 5 px if that is more), and are the canvas's own
    everywhere else, to the bit, with the tooth measured as the canvas's
    alone. Where the part worked out again ends, the two meet as they
    are, and within the maps' reach of the edge they are not the same
    there: the canvas's own maps take its border as going on past the
    edge, the part worked out again has the weave going on, as along
    every edge the canvas's maps and those of what lies past it differ. So
    a map can step along that line by as much as it does across the
    edge: `peaks_wide` by 0.15 units on coarse linen, where its largest
    step from one pixel to the next is 0.07, and by 0.012 on a gesso
    panel; the tooth within 18 px of the edge, the other maps as far in
    as they read. (As first made, anything dried past the edge had the whole
    canvas's relief worked out again with what lies round it: a dab dried
    past one edge changed the tooth at every pixel, the other maps along
    every edge, and a dry brush pulled across the middle afterwards.)
  - A run of steps that can lay paint past the edge is painted on a stage
    made when the run begins and gone when it ends, the canvas keeping
    what it held past the edge; a long run is cut into stages of about
    64,000 px, one after another. A knife is painted all its way, however
    far out. A brush's stages end 128 px out: further out it goes on as
    over the stage's edge and uses its paint up there, as it did past the
    canvas's edge, so that what it leaves within 128 px does not hang on
    how far out it started. (A flat brush 60 px wide pulled on from 80,
    100, 120, 160, 200 and 400 px out leaves within 128 px of the edge
    what it leaves there on a larger canvas, within 0.2%, 0.7% from 400 px
    out, and a clean knife pulled on after it brings on as much, within
    0.2%, 1% from 400 px. What reaches the canvas from 400 px out is 6 to
    8% short, 10% before: the brush runs low out there otherwise than over
    bare canvas, `brush_far.py` of the change.)
  - What the canvas keeps is bounded: what a stroke leaves further out
    than 128 px is forgotten once no later step of the stroke can reach
    it. (128 px by measurement: at 64 px a brushed ground running 40 px
    over the edge is not laid out there, and the first pull below brings
    on 2,510 units for 4,370; at 256 px nothing below changes.)
  - After a stroke whose reach crosses the edge, the paint settles on
    pieces of the canvas and what lies past it together, from the paint as
    the stroke left it: a strip along each edge in reach, 40 px of the
    canvas and all that is kept past the edge there, cut along its length
    into pieces of about 64,000 px that overlap by 40 px, each put back 20
    px in from where it was cut (the settling of a pixel hangs on nothing
    further than 16 px from it), so that no pixel is put back twice; the
    rest of the canvas settles as ever. A stroke that reaches no deeper
    than 160 px into the canvas (one along an edge) settles in one such
    strip that holds all of it, and nothing settles twice. A piece is
    gone when it has settled; what a later piece reads of it is kept as
    the stroke left it, 20 px wide. Whether any paint is thick enough to
    level (`_engine.level` asks it of what it is given) is asked of all
    the pieces and the canvas together. What comes out does not hang on
    how the settling is cut, to the bit: in pieces of 64,000, 16,000 and
    1,500 px, in strips with the canvas settling alone or all in one
    strip, the same canvas and the same paint past its edge
    (`test_the_paint_settles_the_same_however_it_is_cut`). The strips,
    with the canvas settling alone, need 80 px of canvas between two
    opposite edges, four times the 20 px; they are used only where the
    stroke reaches deeper than 160 px, and an assertion keeps that depth
    no less than the 80. With the stages the strokes are
    painted on cut smaller as well (64,000, 16,000, 5,000 and 1,500 px),
    ten strokes at three edges leave the same paint within 7e-5 units on
    the canvas and 5e-5 past its edge.
  - One kernel is changed: what a brush meets round its window
    (`_engine._meet_box`) is read no further than what the step is painted
    on reaches (the box the stroke can reach now goes on past the canvas).
    What served the knife's old way of going on past the edge, which no
    step takes any more, is gone: the kernels `off_surface`, `crop_taps`
    and `nearest`, the copy `blade_rest` gathered for it, and the
    parameters of `blade_slab` and `blade_ends` that said what lay past
    the edge. Knife strokes on the canvas come out as before, to the bit.
  - The weave past the edge is the canvas's own over again, not its
    mirror image, whose lay is turned over: skimming through a corner at
    pressures 0.03 and 0.1 a blade laid 7% and 4% less on the canvas than
    on larger canvases (six weaves each, which differ by 2% among
    themselves) over the mirrored weave, and 2% less over the weave
    repeated (it was 20% and 34% less).

- **Breaking, in huile's pictures:** a knife at the edge of the canvas no
  longer drains the wet paint there, and what it lays there no longer hangs
  on its steps (murex-duo). This was the first step, and the entry above
  has since replaced what it put past the edge: what follows is the record
  of what was wrong before it, of what it did and of what was measured
  then, not of how a canvas is now. The steps of a knife's stroke whose
  windows reach past the edge were painted on a stage (`_knife._Stage`,
  `_Edge`): the part of the canvas under them, and the canvas going on
  past its edge as its own mirror image, wet paint, dried paint and weave,
  as it was before the stroke, up to 128 px out. On the stage a step is a
  step like any other, with canvas all round; when those steps are done
  the stage's part of the canvas is put back, and the rest of the stroke
  is painted on the canvas. A knife whose windows all lie on the canvas is
  painted on the canvas itself and comes out as before, to the bit.
  Brushes were not changed.

  What was wrong. The flow under a dragging blade
  (`_engine.flow_under_blade`, `flow_under_riding_blade`) was given the
  canvas alone: each pixel passes paint on to its neighbour downstream, so
  at the canvas's edge paint went out, uncounted, and none came in. A blade
  22 px wide pushed in steps of 1 px over 2.4 units of wet paint leaves
  1.84 units behind it; coming on over the edge it left 0.38 to 1.28 units
  in the first 10 px, and going off it lost 47 units per row of the blade.
  Past the edge the blade met the canvas's edge pixel, repeated and still,
  whatever it had done there. So a blade coming on, or set down across the
  edge, found the paint under it drained and laid its own in its place,
  more or less of it as its stroke was cut into steps. Measured with the
  same stroke on a canvas 90 px larger all round, the same even ground on

  as more tall lumps: on the dark ground of `knife_probe.py ribs`, 48
  pixels stand above 8 units where 18 did (body 0.7; 34 against none at
  body 0.2), a few small glints in the picture (murex-6fa).
- **Breaking, in fusain's pictures:** how many points a path is cut into, and

  whole numbers in exact arithmetic and that rounding leaves a hair either
  side of: a line 1500 px long sampled every 2.4999999999999996 px (as fusain

  `value_noise_1d`. The fix is oilpaint's (joce/oilpaint#28), which had it in
  its own copies of these modules; with one copy, fusain gets it too.
  Measured:

  - fusain: every hash of `tools/fusain/fingerprint.py` is as it was, the

- **Breaking, in huile's pictures:** a knife barely touching (pressure under

  1.2 below that, so a blade carrying more than twice that of paint lifted its

  that (murex-iaa). The same pulls now leave 2928, 9693, 16785, 18938 and

  canvas, a thin and a thick wet base, what a pull leaves rises with the load

  bounded) (`tools/huile/knife_probe.py skim`). Between pressures 0.06 and

  on top of it: about twice as thick at pressure 0.08, load 8. Of the 16 scenes of

  `knife-lift` (0.82%, the two strokes laid at pressure 0.2; 215/255 at most)
  and `painting` (0.54%, 109/255 at most). The others are identical.
- `spectral` derives `SPECTRUM_TO_RGB` and its basis spectra when
  `WAVELENGTHS` is given another number of bands than the 24 the stored
  tables are for (an experiment: the pictures are checked with 24). huile's
  check that its kernels were compiled for that number is
  `murex/huile/_bands.py`.
- `repr` of a `Pigment` names its class, so that of a `Paint` reads
  `Paint(...)`.

- `spectral.SPECTRUM_TO_RGB` and the basis spectra behind
  `srgb_to_reflectance` are stored as literals (`murex/_spectral_tables.py`),
  no longer worked out at import by NumPy's interpolation, matrix products and
  `np.linalg.solve`, whose last bits differ from one NumPy, BLAS or processor to
  another. The literals are the values the code gave under NumPy 2.5 on x86-64,
  to the last bit, so on x86-64 under NumPy 2 nothing changes (every public
  function of `spectral` returns the same bits as before, and the 325 drawings
  of fusain's thorough fingerprint hash the same). **Breaking elsewhere, in the
  last bits:** where NumPy, BLAS or the processor derived other tables (an ARM
  machine, another BLAS), the results now follow the stored tables instead.
  The derivations are kept (`spectral._build_spectrum_to_rgb`
  and `_build_basis`, and `tools/derive_spectral_tables.py`, which writes the
  module); a test checks that they agree with the tables to 1e-13.

- **Breaking, for anyone on NumPy 1.x:** NumPy 1.x is no longer supported. The
  requirement is `numpy>=2.0`, and numba's floor rises with it, from
  `numba>=0.57` to `numba>=0.60` (numba 0.57 to 0.59 do not support NumPy 2).
  The reason: `spectral.srgb_to_reflectance` returned different numbers under
  NumPy 1.26.4 and under NumPy 2.5.5 (same Python 3.12, numba 0.68.1 and
  murex 0.2.0; the reflectance of a colour hashed differently), and two
  libraries depend on these numbers, one of which checks its pictures byte for
  byte. CI and the lock only ever ran NumPy 2. No result under NumPy 2
  changes.

### Added

- `tests/test_layers.py` checks which way imports point: colour (`spectral`,
  `pigment`) imports nothing else of murex, the other shared modules import
  no medium, a medium imports no other medium, and importing `murex` imports
  no medium. `tests/test_rounding.py` nudges lengths by an ulp and checks that
  the counts stay.
- `tests/test_bits.py` pins reflectances, spectrum to RGB and Kubelka-Munk
  layers to the last bit, on x86-64. On ARM eight of them differ in the last
  bits, so on another kind of processor they are skipped.

### Known, not changed

- Some results still follow the processor to the last bit: NumPy's `power` and
  `tanh` on arrays take a SIMD path on processors with AVX-512 (`tanh`, one
  without AVX2) that rounds some values differently from libm, so `srgb_to_linear`, `linear_to_srgb`, `solve_absorption` (and the optics
  of a `Pigment`) are not the same on every machine, and a BLAS with fused
  multiply-add sums `linear_rgb_to_reflectance` differently from one without.
  The media's kernels, their random draws and `noise.py` follow the machine
  likewise (numba's `math` is the C library's). A picture is therefore the
  same to the bit on one machine, and may differ in its last bits on another.
  Making every machine agree was done and undone before any release
  (`murex-krb`, `murex-9v4`): it takes murex's own maths library and random
  draws, about 900 lines with NumPy's ziggurat tables, and a ban on ordinary
  NumPy calls in murex's code, which is more than this project is worth.
  Recorded hashes are those of the machine that recorded them; fusain's
  fingerprint test and the pins of `tests/test_bits.py` skip elsewhere.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-15

Faster, with every result as it was: nothing here is a breaking change. What
`spectral` and `spline` return is the same to the last bit as in 0.1.1 for the
same arguments, which `tests/test_exact.py` checks against the code of 0.1.1.

### Added

- `spectral.km_layer` works out a batch of layers in compiled code: `kappa`
  (..., bands) and `sigma` (...) float64 arrays of two dimensions or more,
  over one ground (bands,) or over a ground for each layer (..., bands), as
  when every pixel of a picture has its own layer over what is already seen
  there. Five to six times as fast as numpy for a sheet of 1120 by 820
  pixels. Any other arguments are worked out by numpy, as before.
- `spectral.km_layer(..., out=)`: an array to put the result in, which may be
  the ground itself (a layer laid over what is seen, in place).

### Changed

- A `Path` works out its dense points once, at the first `sample()` or
  `length`, and keeps them; they were worked out at every call. It works
  them out again if its control points (or `alpha`, or `closed`) change, in
  place or not; a path whose `alpha` is an array keeps nothing. A subclass
  that implements `_dense` in its own way says what
  its dense points depend on in `_state`, or they are worked out at every
  call, as before.
- The dense points of a `CatmullRom`, the lengths along a path and the
  tangents of `sample()` are worked out in compiled code. Sampling the path
  of a short stroke and asking its length takes a tenth of the time.
- Where numpy warned of a result that is not a number (layers that are not
  finite, a Catmull-Rom path whose knots run into each other), the compiled
  code gives the same result without the warning.

## [0.1.1] - 2026-11-12

The first release. There is no 0.1.0: an unrelated project that once had this
name on PyPI used that version number in 2017, and PyPI never lets a file name
be used twice.

### Added

- The modules oilpaint and fusain share, taken from oilpaint as they are:
  `spectral` (colour science and Kubelka-Munk mixing), `spline` (stroke paths
  and profiles), `noise` (textures), `reference` (reference images), `png`
  and `filters` (oilpaint's `_filters`, with public names).
- `Pigment`: oilpaint's `Paint` without what is particular to oil paint
  (its body).
